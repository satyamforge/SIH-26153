🛡️ NETRA — Everything in Simple Language
1. What is NETRA?

NETRA = Network Attack Forecasting System.

Simple explanation:

“NETRA looks at network traffic, checks whether the traffic looks suspicious, and then estimates whether the attack risk is going to increase in the next few minutes.”

One line to memorize:

“NETRA doesn't just detect attacks; it forecasts attack risk.”

2. What problem are we solving?

Normal cybersecurity systems are often reactive.

For example:

Hacker attacks
      ↓
System detects attack
      ↓
Alert
      ↓
Administrator reacts

The problem is that by the time the attack is detected, damage may already be happening.

NETRA tries to change this:

Network traffic
      ↓
Analyze patterns
      ↓
Detect suspicious behavior
      ↓
Forecast future risk
      ↓
Warning
      ↓
Administrator prepares
Tell the judges:

“Our objective is to move from reactive security toward proactive security.”

3. What is network traffic?

This sounds complicated but it isn't.

Imagine the internet as a road.

Cars = network connections.

NETRA observes characteristics of those connections.

For example:

How long the connection lasted
How many packets were sent
How large those packets were
How frequently packets were sent
Source and destination information
Other flow-level characteristics

These characteristics become the input for our AI model.

4. What dataset did we use?

We used:

CIC-IDS2017

You can simply say:

“We trained our prototype using the CIC-IDS2017 dataset, which contains normal network traffic as well as different types of cyberattack traffic.”

The dataset contains examples of attacks such as:

DoS

Infiltration

Don't try to memorize all of them.

Just remember:

“Normal traffic + attack traffic.”

5. What happens to the dataset?

This is the easiest way to explain it:

Raw Network Traffic
        ↓

        ↓

        ↓

Our dataset has many network-flow characteristics.

After preprocessing, our prototype uses 70 features for the trained model.

If a judge asks:

“Why preprocessing?”

Answer:

“Real-world data can contain missing, invalid, or non-numerical values, so we clean and convert the data before giving it to the machine-learning model.”

6. What AI model are we using?
Random Forest

Don't explain the mathematics.

Tell them:

“Random Forest is a machine-learning algorithm that uses many decision trees together to make a more reliable classification.”

Think of it like:

Decision Tree 1 ──┐
Decision Tree 2 ──┤
Decision Tree 3 ──┤──→ Final Decision
Decision Tree 4 ──┤
Decision Tree 5 ──┘

Many trees vote together.

Judge asks: Why Random Forest?

Say:

“Because our network data is structured tabular data with many features, and Random Forest is effective for this type of classification and is practical for our prototype.”

7. What does the model actually predict?

This is VERY important.

Our current model does:

Network Traffic
      ↓
       AI
      ↓
BENIGN or ATTACK

But internally we use the probability from the model.

So instead of only:

Attack ❌

we can get something like:

Attack probability = 63%

That probability is what we use for forecasting.

8. What does "attack probability" mean?

Suppose NETRA says:

10%

It means:

“The current traffic pattern has a relatively low estimated attack risk.”

Suppose it says:

75%

That means:

“The current traffic pattern appears much more suspicious and should be monitored.”

It's not saying there is exactly a 75% chance a hacker will attack.

It's better to say:

“The model's estimated attack risk is 75% based on the observed traffic pattern.”

That's a much safer technical explanation.

9. Where does FORECASTING come in?

This is the most important part of your project.

Imagine we have:

Minute 1 → 5%
Minute 2 → 6%
Minute 3 → 7%
Minute 4 → 8%
Minute 5 → 9%

The risk is increasing.

NETRA looks at this recent trend and estimates what might happen next.

For example:

Current
   ↓
6.3%

Future
   ↓
7.2%
7.7%
8.3%
8.8%
9.3%
That's the forecasting part.

Say:

“We use the recent sequence of attack probabilities to estimate the direction of future risk.”

10. Is this an LSTM?

NO.

Don't let your teammates accidentally say:

“We use LSTM.”

We don't.

Our current prototype uses:

Random Forest + temporal trend forecasting

That's completely okay for the prototype.

If judges ask:

“Why didn't you use LSTM?”

Answer:

“For the current prototype we chose a simpler and faster forecasting approach so that the system can demonstrate the complete detection-to-forecast pipeline. A more advanced time-series model such as LSTM or Transformer-based forecasting can be added in the next stage.”

That's actually a good answer.

11. What is a traffic window?

This sounds scary but it's simple.

We don't look at every individual network flow separately on the dashboard.

We group flows together into small sequential groups.

Think:

1000 network flows
       ↓
 One traffic window
       ↓
Calculate average attack probability

Then:

Window 1 → 4%
Window 2 → 5%
Window 3 → 6%
Window 4 → 8%
...

Now we can see the trend.

12. Why are we using sequential windows?

Because forecasting requires a sequence.

You can't forecast a trend from just one number.

We need:

Past → Present → Future

That's why NETRA organizes predictions into sequential traffic windows.

14. What does LOW / MEDIUM / HIGH / CRITICAL mean?

Our prototype uses thresholds.

Conceptually:

0–30%     → LOW
30–60%    → MEDIUM
60–80%    → HIGH
80–100%   → CRITICAL

So if predicted risk reaches 85%:

🚨 CRITICAL

If it is 70%:

⚠️ HIGH

If it is 15%:

🟢 LOW

15. What does "Trend" mean?

Very simple.

Increasing

Risk is going up.

20 → 30 → 40 → 50
Decreasing

Risk is going down.

70 → 60 → 45 → 30
Stable

Risk isn't changing significantly.

20 → 21 → 20 → 22

If asked:

“Why didn't you train on all 2.8 million rows?”

Answer:

“The full dataset contains over 2.8 million flows, so for the rapid prototype we used a stratified sample of 500,000 rows to reduce training time while maintaining both classes. The production version can use the complete dataset and larger-scale training infrastructure.”
